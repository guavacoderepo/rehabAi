How RehabAI works

A short bedside assessment feeds into a model that estimates the patient's barrier burden, walking probability, and overall walking recovery outlook.

What is the WPI?

The Walking Probability Index (WPI) is a score between 0 and 70 that captures how much the patient's current condition is likely to hinder their recovery of independent walking.

Lower is better. A low WPI means few barriers stand in the way — the patient is well positioned to walk again. A high WPI means more obstacles are present, and recovery is likely to require greater support.

  • 0 - 20 — Low barrier burden. Independent walking is likely.
  • 21 - 45 — Moderate barrier burden. Recovery is plausible with rehabilitation.
  • 46 - 70 — High barrier burden. Significant support may be needed.

WPI is not a diagnosis. It's a decision-support signal meant to sit alongside clinical judgement.

What counts as a "good" WPI?

There is no universal "good" value — it depends on context. In practice:

  • Lower is better for the goal of walking independently.
  • A falling WPI over time is usually a good sign — barriers are reducing.
  • A rising WPI between visits may warrant a review of the care plan.

Walking probability

Walking probability is the chance that the patient will be able to walk without physical assistance within the target time window. It is derived from the WPI and shown as a percentage in the patient view.

  • High walking probability — the patient is likely to regain independent walking.
  • Low walking probability — the patient may need ongoing support or targeted therapy.

Because walking probability moves in the opposite direction to WPI, a low WPI produces a high walking probability.

Barrier burden

Barrier burden summarises how much the patient's current limitations are expected to impede recovery. It combines pain, mobility, strength, and other factors into a single score from 1 to 10.

Scores are grouped into four bands, based on how each patient compares to the reference population:

  • Lower barrier burden — the patient has fewer obstacles than 75% of the reference group. Recovery is likely to progress smoothly.
  • Lower-intermediate barrier burden — some obstacles are present; targeted therapy can help.
  • Higher-intermediate barrier burden — a meaningful number of obstacles; a structured rehabilitation plan is recommended.
  • Higher barrier burden — the patient faces the most obstacles. Intensive support may be needed.

Lower barrier burden is better. The four bands are derived from population quartiles, so a patient's band reflects how they compare to others — not just an absolute score.
